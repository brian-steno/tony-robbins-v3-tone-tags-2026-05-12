Inflation script

This packet uses direct fixed-text TTS renders. It is not the LiveKit runtime smoke page.

Plain text target:

Today I want to talk about the direct consequence of letting bureaucrats control the money. Inflation. And what happens when nobody is held accountable for it.

Let's go back to the schoolyard.

This time, it's a fair. The kids have 100 coins in circulation — coins they earned by doing work. Mowing the teacher's lawn. Stacking chairs. Helping the younger kids. And with those coins, they buy candy at a little stand. There are 100 candies. 100 coins. One candy costs one coin. Everyone knows what their effort is worth. Everyone knows what they can afford. The system is healthy.

Leo works hard. He's got 20 coins. Tom prefers to play — he's got 2. The teacher thinks that's unfair. But this time, she's learned. She's not going to confiscate anything directly. She has a more subtle idea.

She prints more coins. Quietly. She adds 100 new ones into circulation, and gives them mostly to the kids who have the least. Problem solved, right? More equality. And nobody complained.

Except.

There are still only 100 candies. But now there are 200 coins. The kid running the candy stand isn't stupid — he doubles his price. One candy now costs two coins.

Leo's 20 coins? They now buy 10 candies instead of 20. Tom's 2 coins? Still buy one candy. Nobody got richer. But Leo — the kid who saved, who worked hard — just lost half the value of his labor. Silently. Without a debate. Without a vote. Without doing anything wrong.

That is inflation.

Not an act of God. Not a natural disaster. Not Putin's fault. Not the grocery stores.

Inflation is an organized transfer of wealth — from the people who earned and saved the money, to whoever receives the newly printed money first. Engineered by the people who control the printing press.

And who gets served first? Governments drowning in debt. Banks. The last ones in line? Workers. Savers. Regular people.

That means you.

And the teacher? She thinks it's brilliant. She gets to promise candies without ever having to admit she's taking them out of someone else's pocket. She calls it "accommodative monetary policy."

And who actually controls all of this? Christine Lagarde. Jerome Powell. Unelected bureaucrats who have never built a business, never taken a personal financial risk, never been held accountable for failure. They decide the value of the currency for hundreds of millions of people. And their track record is dismal.

Inflation is not an economic policy. It's a hidden tax — and it falls hardest on the poor and the middle class. The people who can't protect themselves by buying gold, investment property, or stocks.

The wealthy own assets — and assets inflate. The poor earn wages — and wages melt.

No bureaucrat — no matter how smart — has the information to make decisions on behalf of millions of people. The teacher doesn't know what a candy should cost. Nobody does. Only the market — through billions of decentralized exchanges happening in real time — can discover the real price.

Money should be a stable measure. Not a political weapon.

And as long as we let unelected officials decide how much of it exists — we'll keep calling theft social progress.
